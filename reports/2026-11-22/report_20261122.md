### 1. TideCache: Efficient Hybrid LLM Serving with Transparent Cache Management and Layer-Wise Streaming

Hybrid architectures have become mainstream among open-weight language models. Their global-attention and bounded-state layers maintain heterogeneous caches whose demands vary with context length and concurrency. We present TideCache, a serving system on CPU--GPU superchips with three components for allocating GPU memory among caches and managing their residency across GPU and host memory. The Transparent Cache Pool redistributes physical pages among cache components behind fixed virtual addresses, preserving cache layouts and backend interfaces. The Cache Streamer uses computation intervals provided by bounded-state layers to prefetch the next global-attention layer's prefix into GPU slots shared across global-attention layers, reducing GPU residency while overlapping layer-wise transfers with computation. Generated KV remains resident. The Request Scheduler selects resident or streamed execution and triggers capacity adjustments according to request size and queue state. Together, they let HBM released by streaming accommodate other cache components and additional requests. Implemented in SGLang and evaluated on GB300 and GH200 across seven model/platform configurations, TideCache completes up to 2.04x as many agent turns, reduces overall P50 per-token latency by 20--59%, and improves agent P50 TTFT by 1.40--3.05x compared with SGLang with HiCache.

### 2. Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher

Acquiring every feature of an instance is often costly: a measurement can carry a monetary price, a time delay, or a privacy cost. Active Feature Acquisition (AFA) addresses this by learning a policy that decides, per instance, which features to acquire under a budget. Existing AFA methods train this policy by optimizing a self-constructed utility target rather than distilling an explicit teacher. We construct this teacher explicitly. At training time the label and the unobserved feature values are available, and together they determine the teacher in closed form: an oracle that ranks every candidate by its effect on the downstream predictor's error. OracleImitator distills this ranking into a deployable policy by soft-target imitation. Across multimodal medical diagnosis and the AFABench benchmark, OracleImitator matches or outperforms existing AFA methods across the budget range, recovering 90% of the attainable accuracy gain at under a quarter of the acquisition cost. Because the teacher is an explicit algorithmic object rather than an implicit optimization target, we can also measure directly how much of its ranking is recoverable by a policy restricted to what is visible at deployment.

### 3. Realized versus Population Reliability: Anytime-Valid Self-Consistency Certification under Correlated Reasoning

Self-consistency certification treats repeated sampling from a language model as sequential evidence accumulation and stops when an anytime-valid e-process crosses a threshold, guaranteeing finite-sample Type I error control by Ville's inequality. ### 5. Text-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs

Multimodal image generation systems have made rapid progress and increasingly emphasize content safety. However, their safety mechanisms tend to rely on a single modality and lack joint consideration of both visual content and text prompts, particularly in instruction-guided image editing, where the uploaded image may itself carry risk. We thus introduce EditSafetyBench, a benchmark that measures system-level safety awareness of advanced image editing systems, containing 5,940 image-instruction pairs, covering safe and unsafe images across 11 risk categories and three edit intents. We then probe the benchmark dataset on four systems, including Gemini, GPT, Qwen API, and Qwen Local. Based on our evaluation, we observe a systematic text-vision safety mismatch: Gemini and GPT refuse 87.1\% and 81.6\% cases containing unsafe-intent editing prompts, while only refusing 24.2\% and 22.4\% cases with both unsafe images and neutral instructions. At the category level, most risks are considered inconsistently by different systems, e.g., GPT allows 70.4\% editing attempts of unsafe political content while Gemini only allows 34.8\%. Notably, sexual content shows the strongest visual sensitivity, likely benefiting from mature NSFW identification works. Our findings highlight the need for safety guardrails that jointly reason over both visual and textual modalities in image editing systems.
