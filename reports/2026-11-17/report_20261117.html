1. Evaluating Cultural Reasoning and Robustness in LLMs for Spanish Political Discourse

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to interpret, generate, and mediate public-facing language, which makes their reliability especially consequential in politically sensitive settings. Although sentiment analysis is a common task in this area, affective meaning in social media discourse is often conveyed through culturally situated and pragmatically indirect cues that are not well captured by label-centric evaluation alone. In this paper, we evaluate the robustness of LLMs for sentiment analysis of Spanish political discourse from social media, with emphasis on culturally situated affective interpretation. We introduce a diagnostic protocol that combines three independent human annotation views into Gold and Ambiguous subsets, and extends the corpus with a Synthetic subset built from sentiment-flipped counterfactuals selected through model-conditioned perplexity and LLM-as-a-judge criteria. Using this framework, we compare proprietary and open-weight models under zero-shot and few-shot prompting, focusing on predictive robustness across human agreement conditions and counterfactual shift. Our results show that annotation ambiguity and counterfactual perturbation reveal failure modes that conventional evaluation on the original corpus tends to obscure. GPT-4.1-mini remains the most stable among the evaluated models, whereas the two smaller open-weight models degrade more markedly under synthetic perturbations, and few-shot prompting provides only limited gains.

2. Severe but Harmless: Stress-Testing Shift Severity for Frozen Time-Series Forecasters

Zero-shot time-series foundation models (TSFMs) have become attractive alternatives to task-specific models because they handle diverse downstream tasks, including anomaly detection and forecasting, without per-series training. However, time-series data undergo distribution shifts, and the dissimilarity between two regimes is commonly scored with statistical measures such as Wasserstein-1, where a higher score is read as a more severe shift. In systematic experiments using three established severity measures on nine regime shifts from three standard traffic datasets, we show that these measures do not reliably predict degradation for instance-normalized TSFMs. The regimes rated most severe are not the regimes that degrade the models most, and degradation reaches 29% in the lowest-scoring regime. In most cases, the audit shows no positive relationship between measured severity and model degradation. We provide an equivariance-based stress-testing analysis that explains why severity readings can become decoupled from model degradation.

3. Reveal: A Provenance-Aware System for Multimodal Urban Data Exploration

Urban sensing initiatives increasingly produce multimodal datasets that combine visual and textual information to capture city characteristics and subjective factors such as safety, comfort, and accessibility. Yet, analyzing such data at scale remains difficult, as manual inspection is unfeasible and automated methods struggle to interpret subjective cues and uncover relationships across diverse data modalities. Moreover, existing workflows rarely make exploration steps explicit, hindering reproducibility. To address these challenges, we introduce Reveal, a provenance-aware visual analytics system for exploring heterogeneous urban data. Reveal enables users to build dataflows by combining text and image searches across coordinated views, chaining queries into pipelines, and applying set operations within an exploration diagram while capturing analytical provenance. We evaluate Reveal through two case studies focused on accessibility and walkability.

5. Latent Lattice Language Models

We instead increase computational capacity: the number of active computational states a model maintains. We introduce the Lattice of Latents, a set of per-layer horizontal latent streams that run recurrently along time, alongside the vertical residual stream that flows through depth. Like hyper-connections, mHC, and attention or value residuals, the Lattice enhances the residual stream, but rather than adding or rerouting residual pathways through depth, it provides companion computational states that persist across tokens.
