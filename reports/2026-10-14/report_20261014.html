1. GHOST: Geometry-Constrained Hybrid Diffusion for Open-Vocabulary 3D Scene Graph Generation

3D scene graph generation requires understanding both object semantics and metric spatial relations. However, existing open-vocabulary methods often struggle with spatial predicates because language similarity reflects semantic co-occurrence rather than explicit geometry. We propose GHOST, a Geometry-constrained Hybrid diffusion framework for Open-vocabulary 3D Scene graph generaTion. GHOST jointly models continuous node geometry and discrete edge predicates, following a geometry-to-constraint-to-predicate denoising process to ensure geometrically consistent relation prediction. It introduces language-grounded constraint induction, which maps predicate embeddings to differentiable geometric constraints that guide both layout refinement and predicate reweighting. A Geometry-Topology Collaborative Denoiser further couples node and edge reasoning through noise-adaptive interaction and orientation-aware modeling. Experiments on 3DSSG demonstrate state-of-the-art performance in both closed-set and open-vocabulary settings, including over 17-point gains over RelationField on spatial predicates.

3. Retrieval Disagreement as a Signal: Diagnosing and Steering Multilingual Dense Embedders

Multilingual dense retrievers place all languages in one shared space so that a question finds relevant documents, regardless of which language they are written in. We show that the same question, asked in different languages, retrieves substantially different documents, and this disagreement is not because of weak language understanding. Each language performs well on its own and ranks candidates consistently with the other languages; divergence appears only when languages share an index. We trace the mechanism: each language keeps its own ranking, but in the shared index, its correct answers are pushed far down, buried under non-relevant documents that are either in the query's own language or in English, depending on the model. We introduce disagreement-guided steering: for each language, we contrast the documents that other languages agree on for a question against those that language retrieves idiosyncratically, and take the difference of their representations as a language-specific direction, added to that language's queries at inference. Against baselines of language-mean removal, subspace removal, and per-language rotation, it most strongly reduces same-language crowding while remaining competitive on recall, trading off against mean removal, which better preserves ranking quality.

4. Imprinting: Knowledge Distillation Can Shift Student Behavior

Knowledge distillation trains student models on teacher-generated responses to transfer task capability, but those responses may also carry behavioral tendencies of their source. We investigate whether the source teacher can shape student behavior beyond the capability that distillation is intended to transfer. We construct harm-accepting and harm-avoiding variants of the same base teacher, use them to generate responses to ordinary reasoning and knowledge questions unrelated to safety, and distill those responses into students with different base models. Students generally acquire task capability while their downstream decisions shift toward the source teacher's safety-pragmatism disposition, a phenomenon we call imprinting. Distillation from the harm-accepting teacher reduces harm avoidance, whereas distillation from the harm-avoiding teacher increases it. Imprinting varies across teacher-student pairs and does not extend uniformly across different forms of safety behavior. Excluding a sparse set of divergence tokens reduces imprinting, whereas response rewriting preserves the source-dependent effect and can introduce additional rewriter-dependent shifts. Post-distillation preference optimization raises harm avoidance but does not eliminate the source-dependent difference. These results show that behavioral tendencies can be intentionally imprinted through the source teacher, even with ordinary task supervision.

5. The Last Mile of Coding Agents for Long-Document Tasks: An Empirical Study on Clinical Trial Protocol Digitization

Clinical trial protocols serve as the source of truth for study design, but are mostly written and used as unstructured PDF documents. Digitizing protocols turns static content into structured, machine-readable data that clinical software can analyze, validate, and execute. Coding agents, which select tools, execute code, and revise their approach as they work, are increasingly applied to such long-document tasks, yet it remains unclear how reliably they turn complete documents into accurate structured outputs. We study this question using protocol digitization as a testbed. We introduce TrialPD-Bench, an expert-annotated benchmark of 150 publicly available protocol PDFs spanning four task categories. Beyond final-answer performance, our evaluation measures evidence recall from agent traces, agent behavior, and cost. Across nine proprietary and open-weight models in five coding-agent harnesses, even the strongest agents do not fully solve protocol digitization. Our analysis further shows that agents usually locate the required evidence but fail in the last mile of transcribing and structuring it. We further develop TrialScribe, a domain-specific harness that equips coding agents with task skills and a verification loop. TrialScribe improves a locally served open-weight model by 6.2-59.4 points across eight metrics, enabling it to match or surpass proprietary models such as GPT-5.6-Sol.

3. Retrieval Disagreement as a Signal: Diagnosing and Steering Multilingual Dense Embedders

多语言稠密检索器把所有语言放进同一个共享空间，好让提问无论用什么语言都能找到相关文档。这篇由4位作者完成的工作显示，同一个问题用不同语言提出会检索到差异显著的文档，而且这种分歧并非源于语言理解能力弱：每种语言单独表现都不错，候选排序也与其他语言一致，分歧只在语言共享同一索引时才出现。作者进一步定位机制——每种语言保留自己的排序，但在共享索引中它的正确答案被压低，被查询自身语言或英文的非相关文档埋没，具体取决于模型。为此他们提出 disagreement-guided steering：对每种语言，把其他语言一致认可的文档与该语言特异检索到的文档作对比，以两者表示之差作为该语言专属方向，在推理时加到该语言的查询上；与语言均值移除、子空间移除、逐语言旋转等基线相比，该方法最强地缓解同语言拥挤，召回率保持竞争力，并与更能保住排序质量的均值移除形成权衡。这意味着多语言检索的评测重心可能从追求语言无关的单一共享空间，转向显式度量并纠正跨语言分歧，受影响的主要是多语言 RAG、跨语言搜索与嵌入模型的评测与部署，纯单语场景则相对有限。
