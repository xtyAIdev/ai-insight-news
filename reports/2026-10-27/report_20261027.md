### 1. Hypergraph-Enhanced Evidence Selection for Long-Context Multimodal Document QA

Long-context multimodal document question answering (QA) has become an important yet challenging task for modern vision-language models (VLMs), as it requires reasoning over evidence dispersed across pages and modalities within limited input budgets. Current pipelines adopt retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to select multimodal evidence for downstream VLMs. However, existing systems mainly rely on static selection rules over fixed-granularity retrieval units such as pages or blocks, resulting in information loss during retrieval and suboptimal performance in downstream reasoning. To alleviate these issues, we formulate the core task as budget-aware evidence selection over structured document evidence and propose HyperDoc, a document RAG framework that constructs compact yet sufficient evidence while preserving document relations. Specifically, HyperDoc constructs a document hypergraph to explicitly capture structural, logical, and cross-modal relationships among multimodal document elements. Over this structured evidence space, HyperDoc performs adaptive cost-aware hyperedge selection based on query intent and input budget. Extensive experiments on three representative benchmarks demonstrate that HyperDoc outperforms various types of state-of-the-art baselines.

### 2. Belief-Conditioned Multi-Agent Actor Critic for Decentralized LLM Collaboration

Recent work has explored optimizing LLM collaboration through Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), but many approaches still rely on predefined workflows, limiting efficiency and scalability. Decentralized LLM collaboration is challenging because agents execute independently with limited communication, share a joint objective, and must infer useful information from communication traces. In this paper, we propose BCMAAC, a Belief-Conditioned Multi-Agent Actor-Critic method that maintains beliefs over contextual information and uses them to facilitate training. Experiments on Minecraft game-playing, web searching and tool-using tasks show that BCMAAC improves task performance and collaborative behavior compared to naive actor-critic baselines. The improvements are particularly pronounced in multi-turn tasks with richer contextual information, where belief modeling provides a clear advantage.

### 3. Knowing When to Escalate: Disagreement Patterns for Uncertainty-Aware Content Moderation

Agentic content-moderation systems increasingly rely on collective judgments from LLM agents, while aggregation methods commonly treat inter-agent disagreement as a reliability problem to be removed through consensus or voting. This framing is inappropriate for value-laden tasks, where disagreement may reflect irreducible value pluralism - a distinction standard uncertainty decompositions cannot recover, since they operate on output distributions and discard the reasoning behind each judgment. We propose Reasoning Trace Divergence Analysis (RTDA), which embeds model-generated justifications from perspective-differentiated agents and classifies each case by justification similarity and conclusion agreement. The resulting four-state taxonomy isolates convergent disagreement, where agents reasoning similarly but concluding differently, as a candidate signature of value-laden conflict. On 1,000 items from the Measuring Hate Speech corpus, this structural signal predicts human annotator disagreement better than magnitude-only and output-only baselines. We contribute a design principle: condition human escalation on the structure of disagreement, not its magnitude alone, with RTDA serving as a post-processing layer for systems that already generate reasoning traces.

### 4. Preserve to Expand: Source-Aware Adaptation and Model Merging for Multilingual LLM-Based ASR

Expanding multilingual ASR systems to new languages is challenging when adaptation must preserve previously supported languages without replaying source-language training data. We study this problem in connector-based Speech LLMs, where a trainable connector maps speech representations into a frozen large language model. We evaluate target-update construction and merging strategies for adapting the connector while preserving source-language performance. In single-target expansion, target-only fine-tuning and standard TIES merging learn new languages but cause severe source-language forgetting, while SSU-Wanda+TIES gives the best source-preserving trade-off. In multi-target expansion, update merging provides stable source retention, whereas recursive expansion can improve target adaptation under favorable orders but is highly order-sensitive. These results show that multilingual ASR expansion requires balancing target acquisition, source retention, and interactions among target-language updates.
