1. Democratizing Cross-Project Knowledge for Transforming Water Governance and Climate Resilience: an Agentic AI Ecosystem

EU-funded projects produce extensive evidence in the form of deliverables, reports, and case studies. However, this knowledge is typically scattered across projects and document structures, making it difficult to locate relevant, contextual information when making decisions. This paper introduces an Agentic AI Ecosystem that consolidates heterogeneous project documentation into a domain-aware knowledge base and provides adaptive access through an agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline. Documents from three EU-funded projects (IMPETUS, NATALIE, and InnWater) are reconstructed into hierarchical section trees and indexed with structured metadata. Retrieval is supported by two domain-specific embedding models fine-tuned on domain data (water and climate). On top of this knowledge base, a ReAct-style controller iteratively orchestrates retrieval actions by allocating retrieval budget across domain collections and accumulating evidence until a stopping criterion is met. In a retrieval benchmark over the corpus, the proposed approach improves Mean Reciprocal Rank by 26.7% (relative) compared to a single-domain embedding baseline, indicating more consistent retrieval of relevant evidence to support cross-project knowledge synthesis and climate-resilient decision-making.

2. AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control

Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. We introduce AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which the policy agent states a local intent alongside each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode against this intent. Our action-level analysis demonstrates the promising capabilities of frontier agents on direct robot control, including closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, reliable semantic grounding across diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation without task-specific adaptation. It also identifies their current limitations, including task-relevant physical execution bottlenecks such as grasping and alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Finally, we validate our critic on a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and release a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions alongside a fine-tuned open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B, whose failure-mode accuracy rises from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling low-cost action-level analysis.

3. Hybrid Retrieval for Government Scheme Search in English and Marathi

Retrieving government schemes requires matching citizens' information needs to formal administrative descriptions, often across languages and terminology. We present a bilingual search system for Maharashtra government schemes and public services that combines BM25 retrieval with sentence-embedding similarity through equal-weight score fusion. The system indexes aligned English-Marathi scheme descriptions and supports multilingual, Marathi-focused, and translation-mediated semantic configurations. Evaluation on manually curated bilingual queries compares BM25, dense retrieval, and hybrid retrieval using IndicSBERT and MahaSBERT. BM25 provides a strong baseline with a mean reciprocal rank (MRR) of 0.864. Hybrid retrieval increases MRR to 0.872 with IndicSBERT and 0.879 with MahaSBERT, whereas the corresponding dense-only systems achieve 0.663 and 0.655. MahaSBERT-based fusion also increases precision from 0.781 to 0.808 while matching BM25 recall at 0.946. These results support the complementary use of lexical and semantic signals in this collection, while showing that improvements over a strong lexical baseline are modest and metric-dependent. The study provides an application-focused analysis of hybrid retrieval for bilingual public-service information access.

4. AutoMaster: Autonomous Post-Training by Making the Same Model a Better Teacher

Task success alone does not specify which behaviors post-training should reinforce. Even successful teacher trajectories can contain unsupported assumptions, unproductive repetition, or superficial verification. We propose Skill-FT (Skill-Guided Fine-Tuning), which uses natural-language skills to suppress undesirable habits and elicit underrepresented effective behaviors during teacher rollouts, then fine-tunes a target model on the resulting trajectories. We introduce AutoMaster, an autonomous post-training system in which an LLM agent develops these interventions through trajectory analysis, skill design, data selection, and feedback from rollouts and target-model evaluation. Across Terminal, Search, and software engineering tasks with 4B and 9B target models, AutoMaster improves mean performance over Vanilla SFT on successful unguided teacher trajectories by 1.42-8.99 percentage points, with the teacher, task pool, training set size, and SFT recipe held fixed. Together with the ablations and trajectory analyses, these results suggest that behavioral shaping can make a fixed model a more effective teacher. We further find that retaining skill instructions during SFT yields higher mean performance than removing them across all six settings, despite their absence at evaluation and identical supervised outputs. These findings suggest that preserving skill context as part of a training demonstration can support better behavioral internalization.

5. GLiDE: A Fast, Calibrated Decision Model Distilled from Reasoning Teachers

Decision models enable fast, confidence-aware decisions by scoring candidate answers in a single forward pass, without generating text or intermediate reasoning. Yet reasoning can substantially improve decision quality, raising the question of whether its benefits can be retained without the cost of generating it. We investigate this question with \textbf{GLiDE}, a family of decision models spanning 0.4B to 27B parameters. Building on Qwen hybrid language models, GLiDE enables bidirectional interactions between the context, question, and candidate answers by removing causal masking from full-attention layers. To transfer reasoning capabilities into single-pass inference, we combine soft-target distillation from multiple teachers, including a reasoning model, with progressive post-training on diverse decision tasks. We further explore parameter-free recurrent depth to increase inference-time computation without generating tokens. Across five model sizes, GLiDE lies on the performance--parameter Pareto frontier of the Hugging Face \DI{}, with each model outperforming evaluated alternatives of equal or smaller size. \dmtwo{} (27B) achieves a hidden-set score of 71.52, with an expected calibration error of 2.0\% and a median latency of 92.9\,ms on a single GPU. Further checkpoint averaging and parameter-free recurrent depth yield GLiDE~27B, which reaches 73.37 with an expected calibration error of 2.1\%.

2. AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control

GPT-6 Astra 等前沿多模态语言智能体已能绕过高层原语、直接用低层数值动作控制机器人，但现有基准只报告回合级成功率，既看不出它们如何成功，也无法定位失败发生在哪里。为此，作者在 12 个操作基准上开展系统的动作级分析，提出 AgentRoboCritic：策略智能体在给出每个动作的同时声明局部意图，critic 则对照该意图评估动作结果与失败模式。分析确认这些智能体具备从局部执行失败中闭环恢复、跨多样场景可靠完成语义落地、以及无需任务特定适配的强零样本操作能力，同时也暴露出抓取、对齐等与任务相关的物理执行瓶颈，且在不同失败模式下的恢复表现并不均衡。作者在 400 条动作的人工标注基准上验证了该 critic，并公开包含 12,844 条标注动作的数据集与微调后的开源 critic Muse-Glimmer-30B，其失败模式识别准确率从 32.58% 提升至 74.24%，使低成本的动作级分析成为可能。这意味着具身智能评测正从“任务是否成功”推进到“如何成功、何处失败”，为直接低层数值动作控制提供可诊断的失败定位依据。

真正的看点不在成功率数字，而在把评测粒度压到单个动作：若 30B 级开源模型就能以低成本复现 critic，失败模式标注就可能从人工瓶颈转为可规模化的训练反馈信号，部署前验证的重心也会随之从回合成功率移向过程级诊断。
