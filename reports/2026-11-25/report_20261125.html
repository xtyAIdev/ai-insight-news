1. Uncertainty-Aware Candidate Generation for Entity Linking in Cyber Threat Intelligence

Entity Resolution (ER) pipelines often collapse ambiguity into early hard decisions: upstream Named Entity Recognition (NER) selects a single span and type, and downstream Entity Linking (EL) treats that choice as ground truth. This discards valuable information about the ambiguity of the underlying data (e.g., span boundary disagreement and type confusion), leading to brittle systems that fail unpredictably when faced with uncertainty in inputs. Therefore, this work presents a proof-of-concept for uncertainty-aware ER that treats uncertainty as native data that should be preserved and exploited throughout ER pipelines. We (i) capture uncertainty signals in the NER stage and (ii) leverage these signals into the EL stage to improve candidate retrieval under ambiguity. 2. DiaCap: Jointly Learning Who Speaks When for Audiovisual Dialogue Captioning

We present DiaCap, an audiovisual LLM for comprehensive dialogue captioning, which produces the transcript, timestamps, and a visual description of the speaker for every utterance in a video. Considering that utterance timing and audiovisual diarization are tightly coupled, DiaCap unifies the tasks in a single model, trained in two complementary stages. First, we train DiaCap on 225K curated clips, using synthetic captions that densely label every utterance in detail. Second, we further refine the model via reinforcement learning on 2.9K human-corrected clips, using fine-grained feedback that directly evaluates utterance timing, speaker grouping, and speaker binding. On DiaDemBench, which we extend with utterance-level timestamps and subtitle redaction, both DiaCap with off-the-shelf transcripts and its end-to-end variant DiaCap-E2E achieve strong results. Specifically, DiaCap performs on par with Gemini 4 Argon in audiovisual diarization while clearly outperforming it in timestamp accuracy (76.59 vs. 42.76 TIME). It also maintains strong diarization on multilingual and minute-long videos, and transfers to audiovisual question answering.

3. CoT-Proxy: Cross-Format Transfer for Distilling Long-Horizon Policies

Reasoning models become long-horizon agents through reinforcement learning or, more cheaply, through fine-tuning on a stronger teacher's trajectories, which contain the teacher's chain-of-thought as well as its actions. The standard fine-tuning approach imitates both, yet the strongest teachers are closed models whose chain-of-thought is hidden behind an API. We show that the chain-of-thought can be left out altogether and that the long-horizon policy can be distilled from the teacher's actions alone. The student is trained on these trajectories with its reasoning block left empty, while a KL loss on a replay buffer of its own generations limits drift from its initial model. Surprisingly, the policy learned from these trajectories with an empty reasoning block carries over to inference with thinking enabled, where the student reasons in its own style while acting like the teacher, an effect we call cross-format transfer. Distilling the actions of Qwen3.6-27B raises the task success of Qwen3-4B on AppWorld from 7% to 18% and that of Qwen3-32B from 12% to 25%. We further introduce CoT-proxies, short summaries of the teacher's reasoning placed after the empty reasoning block, which raise Qwen3-4B further, to 35%. Since neither actions nor CoT-proxies need the hidden chain-of-thought, the recipe applies unchanged to closed teachers. 5. SAG-Mem-1.0 Technical Report

Long-term conversational memory requires retrieving complementary facts scattered across dialogue sessions, including intermediate evidence with limited similarity to the original question. Existing approaches often rely on derived memory representations or persistent relational structures, introducing construction costs and potentially losing source details. We present SAG-Mem, a conversational memory framework that preserves complete dialogue messages in non-overlapping chunks and discovers complementary evidence through query-time lexical expansion. Memory construction requires no model inference, using a lexical index without LLM-based extraction, summarization, or semantic relation construction. At query time, BM25 retrieves initial candidates, an evidence evaluator identifies useful chunks, and lexical cues from selected evidence guide an additional retrieval round. Retrieved dialogue is further annotated with calendar interpretations of supported relative-time expressions, preserving the original wording and temporal granularity. We also introduce SAG-Mem-Lite, an alternative configuration that replaces evidence scoring with embedding-based candidate scoring, achieving lower operational cost than Full Context and all memory-based baselines across both benchmarks with low query latency.
