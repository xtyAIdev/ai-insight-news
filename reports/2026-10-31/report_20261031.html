1. VeriCUA: Verification as Agentic Exploration for Computer-Using Agents

Building and scaling computer-using agents (CUAs) across desktop, mobile, and web environments requires reliable task-completion verification for data curation, evaluation, and reinforcement learning. Typical automated approaches prompt vision-language models (VLMs) to judge trajectories or generate programs to inspect application state. However, both rely on partial views of complex digital environments. VLM judges are restricted to supplied observations, while generated programs depend on accessible state. These views can omit decisive evidence or leave completion judgments unsupported. Tailoring separate verifiers to different platforms and access settings adds further engineering cost. We present VeriCUA, a platform-agnostic framework for verification through agentic exploration. It treats verification as dynamic evidence gathering and assessment within a shared procedure for online and offline settings across platforms and observation modalities. After inspecting the available context to construct a task rubric, the verifier selects GUI or code checks for unresolved requirements and combines their findings under explicit evidence rules. Experiments show improved agreement with reference labels over the compared baselines across these settings, with the largest offline gains on hard cases and stronger rejection of false completion claims online. Further analyses connect these gains to complementary evidence and test-time exploration.

2. PIVOT: Progressive Intervention via Online Teacher Correction

Reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) is limited by sparse outcome-level feedback on hard reasoning tasks: when all rollouts in a group fail, rewards provide no learning signal. Stronger teachers can provide additional guidance, but this raises a central question: how can such signals be incorporated into RLVR to support successful rollouts while remaining learnable for the student? We distinguish two complementary mechanisms: trajectory-level intervention, which improves access to reward-bearing trajectories, and token-level supervision, which provides dense targets to guide student learning. Our controlled experiments show that either mechanism alone can remain insufficient, motivating their combination. We therefore introduce PIVOT, which inserts teacher-generated corrections into student rollouts and applies token-level KL supervision to these segments alongside the RLVR objective. Beyond their combination, we systematically analyze where to intervene and how to supervise, yielding a recipe for effective guidance. Across hard logic games and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, PIVOT consistently outperforms existing methods, nearly doubling mean avg@8 on logic games from 19.5% for the best-performing baseline to 35.6%.

3. Every Token Needs Its Own Teacher: Multi-teacher Adaptive On-policy Self-distillation

On-policy Self-distillation (OPSD) has proven effective for post-training large language models by constructing a teacher conditioned on privileged references. However, existing approaches keep the teacher fixed either throughout training by consistently exposing it to the full reference, or within each response by adaptively varying partial reference exposure only across responses. In this paper, we argue that neither of them is sufficient. Instead, every token needs its own teacher, as the teacher preference can vary token by token within the same response. Through a teacher-intervention pilot study, we validate that the teacher preference differs across tokens, and reveal that a preferred teacher can be identified for each token through majority voting and KL-based selection. Motivated by these findings, we propose Multi-teacher Adaptive On-policy Self-Distillation (MTA-OPSD), the first token-level adaptive OPSD method. Our method constructs a multi-teacher pool consisting of candidate teachers, and enables every token to adaptively select its preferred teacher during training. Experiments with Qwen3 models on mathematical reasoning tasks show that MTA-OPSD consistently outperforms existing OPSD approaches with an average gain of up to $+5.9$ over the base model, highlighting the importance of adapting teacher supervision at the token level. The code is available at https://github.com/xingzhejun/MTA-OPSD.

4. Comparing Human Role-Players and LLM-Simulated Clients in Online Counselling Training: An Analysis of Counselling Patterns

This study investigates the capabilities of Large Language Models to simulate counselling clients in educational role-plays in comparison to human role-players. Initially, we recorded role-playing sessions, where novice counsellors interacted with human peers acting as clients, followed by role-plays between humans and clients simulated by Mistrals Mixtral 8x7b using 4-bit quantization. These interactions were analysed with a counselling communication pattern system at sentence level. We investigated two key questions: (1) to what extent LLM-generated responses replicate authentic conversational dynamics and (2) whether counsellors' communication behaviour differs when interacting with human versus LLM-simulated clients. The findings highlight both similarities and differences in the application of counselling patterns across scenarios, showing the potential of LLM-based role-playing exercises to enhance counselling competencies and to identify areas for further refinement in virtual client simulations.

5. Rethinking Neural Thickets Without Weight Perturbations

这篇由5位作者完成的论文复现了 Neural Thickets 假说报告的增益——该假说认为预训练语言模型位于权重空间中任务专家密集的邻域，随机高斯扰动可以将其揭示出来。但作者发现，这些增益并不需要上述几何解释：仅扰动单个 decoder block 就能恢复大部分提升，而在模型完全冻结、只向输出 logits 叠加随机偏置的情况下，多数推理任务上的表现已追平甚至超过全权重扰动。作者据此提出替代解释，即输出多样性、基于验证集的选择与投票，并将其形式化为 Perturb-Select-Vote（PSV）通用流程：生成扰动预测器、在小验证集上选出高表现成员、再聚合其预测，从而把 Neural Thickets 与采样、self-consistency、verifier-guided selection 等推理期方法联系起来。一阶分析进一步表明，小幅权重扰动主要通过其引发的 logit 偏移起作用；该分析被扩展到视觉领域，logit PSV 在包括对地观测在内的域偏移场景下带来显著提升，而全权重扰动几乎没有额外收益。这意味着被归因于权重空间“邻近专家”的收益可以用输出多样性、选择与投票以更低成本获得，也削弱了以权重几何解释微调收益的因果证据。
