### 1. ERCLIP: Small-Data Adaptation of CLIP for Free-Length Image-Text Retrieval

CLIP's 77-token text encoder truncates detailed image descriptions. We present ERCLIP, a small-data adaptation of CLIP for long-caption image--text retrieval. During training, a long caption is split into sentences that the CLIP text encoder encodes independently; the sentence embeddings are fused by mean pooling or a gated residual Transformer scorer and receive their own contrastive loss. At inference ERCLIP is an unmodified CLIP dual encoder: test captions are still read through the 77-token window, and only training sees the full caption. We find that this fine-tuning erodes CLIP's zero-shot classification, most when the fusion output is not a combination of CLIP embeddings, and add a frozen-CLIP preservation loss on the image and short-caption embeddings. With 20k pairs and one epoch, ERCLIP improves long-caption R@1 over CLIP by 6.5--11.2 points, closing 62--77% of the gap to LongCLIP with 2% of its long captions. Preservation recovers 1.5--2.1 ImageNet zero-shot points (30--42% of the loss) and adds up to 2.4 points of COCO image-to-text R@1, at no inference cost.

### 2. Plausible but Unimplantable: A Trustworthiness Audit of Shape Completion Foundation Models for Cranial Defect Reconstruction

A patient who has lost part of the skull vault needs an implant that fits the defect exactly, and such implants are still drawn by hand in CAD by specialists, which makes each case slow and expensive. The task can be considered as a shape completion problem, so pretrained completion models, and now the first shape completion foundation models, look like a way to remove that manual step. We audited three publicly released pretrained shape completion models, two of them foundation models, applied zero-shot to 239 cranial defects, some carved from public skull datasets and some real post-craniectomy cases. Each output was compared with simply returning the patient's own remaining bone, and tested for whether the predicted material could be placed without intersecting the bone that is still there. On the 210 carved defects none of the three improved on doing nothing, and on the 29 real cases only one did so beyond chance. ### 4. Real PMIDs, Wrong Papers: Harness Design for Biomedical Claim-Verification Agents

We run a controlled ablation on biomedical claim verification (SciFact) with the live PubMed API as the only tool. Holding the backbone and output format fixed, we compare four harnesses: closed-book answering, single-shot retrieval using the claim as the query, a ReAct-style agent that writes its own PubMed queries, and an oracle given the gold abstracts, each with two efficient frontier backbones. ### 5. LongZero: Enhancing Long-Context Reasoning in LLMs from Zero Data

Reinforcement learning (RL) is an effective way to strengthen the long-context reasoning of large language models (LLMs), but it needs training data that are long, diverse, challenging, and verifiable. Human annotation of such data is expensive, naturally occurring data are too scarce to sustain RL, and existing synthesis methods depend on external corpora or stronger teacher models, which become harder to obtain as the trained models get stronger. Recently, RL from zero data, in which a model learns from tasks it proposes itself, has achieved excellent performance, but existing methods target short-context tasks. Therefore, we propose $\textbf{LongZero}$, a method that lets an LLM improve its own long-context reasoning without any external data. Instead of writing questions and answers over existing documents, LongZero first has the model synthesize the question and answer with its supporting evidence in a compact structured form, and only then separately expands the evidence into a long multi-document context. LongZero combines these self-written multi-hop reasoning tasks with self-written multi-round retrieval tasks and trains the model with GRPO. We evaluate LongZero on two LLMs across five long-context reasoning benchmarks and compare it with RL on data synthesized from external corpora. Experimental results show that LongZero can generalize to real tasks, and outperforms both baselines, suggesting a viable and promising path to improving long-context reasoning of LLMs.
