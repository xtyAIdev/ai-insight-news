### 2. Does the Action Matter? Probing Action-Conditioned Semantic Forecasting as a World Model for Autonomous Driving

World models are increasingly used in autonomous driving to predict how a scene evolves under the ego vehicle's actions, supporting counterfactual reasoning and model-based planning. However, existing evaluations focus on prediction fidelity and planning performance, and rarely verify that the predictions depend on the conditioning action. In this paper, we study action dependence in action-conditioned semantic forecasting, which predicts future semantic segmentation from past segmentations and the ego action. We compare a forecaster with feature-wise and input-level action conditioning against an identical model without action input, and measure action dependence with test-time action interventions scored by standard segmentation metrics. ### 3. Jailbreak Detection on Large Language Models via Contrastive Prototype Structures

Jailbreak attempts and harmful prompts remain a challenge for the safe deployment of large language models. We study whether contrastively learned representations can support safety detection without training an additional downstream classifier. We propose Jailbreak Detection with Prototype Inference (JDPI), which uses binary safety labels to construct harmful and benign prototypes and makes predictions from relative prototype distances. JDPI uses only binary safety labels for encoder adaptation, prototype construction, and validation. In our setup, it uses fewer labeled examples than the classifier baseline (Method A) by avoiding the separate split used to train the downstream classifier. Across four benchmarks covering different input formats, contrastive fine-tuning improves JDPI's detection accuracy by 9.1-18.0 percentage points. Using the same encoder, JDPI achieves slightly higher ROC-AUC on one benchmark and stays within 0.017 of the baseline across all four. With dataset-specific adaptation, JDPI exceeds the released Llama Guard 3-8B baseline by 0.173-0.310 in ROC-AUC across the four benchmarks. In multi-turn replay on TomGibbs, JDPI uses accumulated user history as the conversation unfolds, achieving higher conversation-level accuracy than when it uses the current turn alone. Across 12 directed transfers, JDPI outperforms the classifier by an average of 1.59 percentage points in accuracy, demonstrating stronger cross-dataset robustness.

### 4. Who are you? The Personal Imitation Game

Large Language Models (LLMs) now perform strongly on Alan Turing's 1950 Imitation Game, so we propose a harder challenge, the Personal Imitation Game, which asks whether a machine can replicate a specific individual in conversation rather than a generic human. Testing with 30 participants, we establish a human baseline and report the identity signals interrogators rely on: stylometric features, personality traits, and biographical facts. To demonstrate the framework, we design an LLM-based system that plays the game for one of the paper's authors, drawing on an autoethnographic dataset spanning ten years of personal digital communication. In a 20-person deception study, interrogators choose our system over the author's own friends in 7 of 10 rounds, and the author over our system in 13 of 20; neither rate is distinguishable from chance at this sample size.
