1. Orthogonal Norm Growth in Two-Tower Retrieval: A Popularity Mechanism for Unified Discovery

Unified search and recommendation systems may update the same item vectors from both interaction streams. We study how repeated updates can increase an item vector's norm and affect retrieval under an unnormalized dot product. A cosine-only loss makes the output gradient perpendicular to the current item vector - tangent to its constant-norm sphere. Training changes encoder parameters, however, so the realized item-vector movement need not remain tangent. We derive sufficient conditions under which it does; every nonzero update then increases squared norm by its squared length. When the tasks share an item encoder, sequential updates from both can accumulate in the same norm, which raw-inner-product serving can expose for either task. This conditional result is not a theorem about joint SGD. Controlled ablations test these conditions. On MovieLens 32M and Amazon Books, accumulated tangent movement accompanies frequency-norm association and additional head-item exposure under raw-dot rather than normalized-item ranking. In a nonlinear tower combining a per-item ID vector with a shared content encoding, active item movement remains near-tangent. We separate its norm change into the ID-update contribution and other output changes, then use a matched training intervention that removes immediate norm growth caused by the ID-row update. 2. Structure and Lineage Evidence for Vietnamese Statutory Article Retrieval on DRiLL

We study Vietnamese statutory Article retrieval on DRiLL using features for provision identity and local form, same-law support, and citation and lineage evidence. Across five random seeds, selectors sharing candidates, listwise Qwen evidence, and learner settings improve official $F_2$ from 71.77% to 72.91%, with the largest descriptive gain on two-Article questions. Added features account for 18.20% of the proposed selector's total decision-tree split gain, measured as training-objective reduction, mainly from same-law and citation evidence. Cases reveal base provisions and amendments, companion recovery, distractor removal, and failures. These findings motivate research on other datasets, representations, and learners without establishing individual feature effects or requiring LightGBM, hand-crafted scores, or explicit graphs.

3. Refer-CityFlow and M3Track: A Benchmark Dataset and Reference Framework for Language-Driven Multi-Target Multi-Camera Tracking

Referring multi-object tracking enables users to specify targets through natural language, but existing Refer-MOT benchmarks primarily focus on single-camera settings. We formalize Referring Multi-Target Multi-Camera Tracking (Refer-MTMCT), where free-form queries specify identity-consistent trajectories to be recovered across a network of sequential, sparse-overlap cameras. We introduce Refer-CityFlow, a benchmark built on CityFlow's global cross-camera identity annotations and augmented with language queries requiring joint language grounding, cross-camera association, and spatio-temporal reasoning. Refer-CityFlow covers 96 videos from 40 roadside cameras with 660+ identity-consistent cross-camera trajectories, 4,362 trajectory-level descriptions, and 7,699 queries, including 1,699 real-user queries collected from 50 participants. Reference experiments on the four-camera Scenario 02 show that single-camera referring trackers reach 14.95-18.28 HOTA on real-user queries, while the evaluated cross-view method achieves about 0.05 HOTA in direct transfer. To establish reference performance, we provide M3Track, a modular multimodal reference framework in which a large language model (LLM)-based agent orchestrates interchangeable detection, tracking, and cross-camera association modules. M3Track reaches 44.10 HOTA and 65.40 IDF1 on the same real-user query set. The project repository is available at https://github.com/phuongnguyen194/Refer-CityFlow-and--M3Track.

4. FlowMoP: Stochastic Multi-Person Motion Prediction

Predicting the future motion of multiple people is inherently stochastic: the same observed history admits many plausible continuations depending on intent, coordination, and social context. Yet the dominant paradigm in multi-person 3D motion prediction remains deterministic, with existing methods producing a fixed number of outputs per agent. In contrast, we present a conditional flow matching framework that jointly generates diverse future motions for multiple agents, modeling both global trajectories and root-relative poses. A B-spline reparameterization compresses the generative state space while enforcing temporal smoothness, and a dual-stream transformer conditions each agent's predictions on observed neighbor motion for interaction-aware forecasting. Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on CMU-Mocap (UMPM) and 3DPW, outperforming deterministic baselines on joint position error, pose error, and final displacement error despite producing a full predictive distribution. We additionally establish the first motion prediction baseline on WorldPose, a large-scale professional soccer dataset, where social conditioning demonstrably concentrates predictive uncertainty along interaction-constrained directions.

5. Flow-Guided Target-Space Alignment via Path Consistency

Many multimodal alignment problems require mapping a source modality into a fixed space defined by a target modality, where the target space is not merely an intermediate embedding but is used directly for downstream analysis. In this setting, successful alignment should preserve both the global structure of the target distribution and the instance-level correspondence observed in paired source--target training data. Existing objectives typically address only part of this goal: contrastive losses encourage relative similarity, distribution-matching losses improve marginal agreement, and pointwise regression fits each source sample to its paired target without explicitly modeling target-space path geometry. We propose FlowAlign, a flow-guided target-space alignment method that repurposes conditional flow matching as a training-time alignment signal rather than an inference-time transport model. FlowAlign uses the learned flow field to guide the alignment of source-side representations with the fixed target space, encouraging them to preserve paired correspondence while remaining compatible with the global target-space structure.
