### 2. Efficient Edge-aware Attention Network for Graph Generation

Graph generative models commonly use graph-specific Transformer architectures to jointly update node and edge features. However, these architectures do not directly benefit from recent hardware-aware attention implementations, limiting their scalability. The adaptation is, however, non-trivial: edge and graph-level features must be injected into pairwise attention scores, graph masks must be respected, and dynamic edge updates remain a major source of quadratic memory usage. We propose an edge-aware attention layer for graph generation that incorporates structural information through head-wise score modulation, while updating edge representations with a lightweight residual mechanism that retains attention-score dependent edge updates. This layer can be effectively implemented using PyTorch's FlexAttention. We achieve similar generation quality to our baseline while reducing peak GPU memory by around 65% and substantially improving training and sampling throughput. These results suggest that the proposed architecture is a practical step toward scalable graph foundation models.

### 3. NarraSpace: Multimodal Scene-Grounded Evidence for Picture-Description Dementia Classification

Picture-description dementia classification uses a shared visual scene, but a transcript-level prediction alone does not identify the scene evidence underlying its inputs. NarraSpace links words and speech times to named picture elements and retrieves utterances for predefined scene events. Narrative-spatial descriptors summarize coverage and temporal organization. A frozen language model contrasts each event's text under examples from Alzheimer's disease and healthy-control training participants, alongside transcript surprisal and coverage measurements. Retained source records link event scores to their source utterances, speech intervals, and lexical matches. On the official 48-participant test set of the Alzheimer's Dementia Recognition through Spontaneous Speech benchmark, a fixed support vector classifier achieves $80.88\%\pm2.56\%$ mean accuracy over fifty seeds; averaged probabilities yield $81.25\%$ accuracy and $91.32\%$ area under the receiver operating characteristic curve. NarraSpace makes the construction of scene-linked classification inputs explicit and inspectable by connecting event-indexed language-model measurements to their source utterances, speech timing, and extraction records. Such source-linked measurements could support clinician review of picture descriptions by presenting quantitative summaries alongside the original speech evidence.

### 4. Benchmarking Arabic Dialects for Math Problems

Mathematical reasoning remains underexplored for low-resource languages, including Dialectal Arabic. To bridge this gap, we introduce the DAR-MGSM (Dialectal Arabic Multilingual Grade School Math) Benchmark, the first manually curated benchmark for evaluating mathematical reasoning in Dialectal Arabic. DAR-MGSM consists of math word problems (MWPs) requiring multi-step reasoning and covers five major Arabic dialects alongside Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). The benchmark comprises two parallel versions: translated-only and translated &amp; culturally localized. The latter version adapts entities and references to regional varieties. We evaluate eleven Arabic-centric and multilingual large language models on DAR-MGSM. The results show that the performance generally declines on the localized version, highlighting the challenges introduced by culturally grounded adaptations. However, the magnitude of this effect varies across models, with Arabic-centric models exhibiting different levels of sensitivity to localization. A mechanistic interpretability analysis shows that translated and localized variants of the same MWPs often induce distinct internal reasoning trajectories, even when aggregate accuracy remains similar. Overall, our results show that mathematical reasoning performance depends on both linguistic variety and cultural context.

### 5. Capable Teacher, Myopic Scorer: Reward-Free Teacher Repair for Agentic On-Policy Distillation

Agentic on-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student agent on its own trajectories while a teacher scores every token. A natural explanation of where it goes wrong is compounding error: the student's mistakes carry its trajectories beyond the region where the teacher is reliable, so recent methods keep training inside that region, through episode-length curricula, teacher-guided turns, prefix replay, or teacher intervention where the two disagree. We show this explanation is incomplete. From the states where the student departs most from the teacher, the teacher's own takeovers still finish the task, yet the student trained on its token scores keeps running out of turns. We attribute this to the myopia of next-token scoring. The teacher rates each token without seeing where the episode ends, so its scores let through the safe, delaying moves that outcome-based RL would punish with zero reward, a penalty agentic OPD lacks.
