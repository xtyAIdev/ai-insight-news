1. From AI-Enabled Remote Patient Monitoring Toward Agentic Robotics: Embodiment, Governance, and Care Integration

AI-enabled remote patient monitoring is evolving beyond passive data collection toward agentic AI systems that interpret patient states, coordinate care, and initiate action across domestic and clinical settings. This structured scoping review finds that mature agentic care has not yet emerged: Most systems remain human led, while stronger decision-action coupling and embodied AI remain largely confined to prototypes and pilots. Healthcare robotics makes the transition from monitoring to action especially visible by translating AI-generated recommendations and decisions into speech, movement, prompting, physical assistance, escalation, and care coordination, making embodiment, clinical integration, human oversight, and governance fundamental design requirements. Further progress requires safe and interoperable care architectures in which intelligent components operate through clinically validated and accountable workflows. We propose agentic robotics as a future direction for supervised systems embedded in the care ecosystem, connecting AI agents and robots with clinicians, caregivers, and patients through auditable workflows that can be interrupted and remain subject to human oversight and explicit AI governance.

2. Do Verifier Judgments Survive Abstraction? Measuring Verification Transport for LLM Agent Evaluation

LLM-based verifiers are increasingly used to evaluate agent executions, but their reliability is typically measured under a single representation of the trajectory. In deployed agent systems, the same execution may be exposed through composed tools, delegation boundaries, or compacted histories, changing what execution evidence is directly observable to the verifier. It therefore remains unclear whether correct verifier judgments persist across such abstraction boundaries. We introduce Verification Transport, a reliability test that measures judgment stability under abstraction-induced changes in execution observability. We construct matched native, abstracted, and restored views of the same frozen executions while holding the task, realized trajectory, outcome, ground truth, evaluation criterion, and verifier fixed. Across four agent-evaluation benchmarks, seven verifier backbones, four verification protocols, and three abstraction boundaries, we find substantial transport failure, reaching about 31% under macro abstraction and delegation. Verification protocols exhibit distinct failure modes: direct verification more often produces incorrect verdicts, whereas evidence-grounded and deliberative protocols tend to abstain under limited observability. This suggests that lower transport failure rates do not necessarily imply greater reliability. 3. BAL: Bidirectional Autoregression in Latent Space for Learning Human Movement Representations

Learning semantically meaningful human movement representations from 3D pose sequences is essential for human behavior modeling. Existing self-supervised methods derive supervision from artificial assumptions, e.g., invariance to augmentation within a certain range or recoverability of masked regions at a certain temporal scale, introducing heuristic hyperparameters that make the learned semantics sensitive to their settings. We propose BAL, a self-supervised framework that instead exploits supervision intrinsic to human movement: the natural ordering structure of everyday behavior. BAL autoregressively predicts in a semantically abstract latent space, analogous to how humans anticipate others' actions rather than their precise joint configurations. Bidirectional autoregressive prediction, both forward and backward in time, fully leverages these ordering constraints while enabling representation extraction using both past and future context at inference. To suppress representation collapse, BAL decomposes each prediction target into a global component capturing sequence-level semantics and a local component capturing context-independent movement. We prove that the local targets retain nontrivial angular diversity unless the representations are completely collapsed, preserving a meaningful training signal. 4. RACE: Ranking-Uncertainty Aware Capacity-Adaptive Customization for Efficient Sequential Recommendation

Efficient sequential recommendation has emerged as a promising paradigm for providing real-time personalized service. To deploy models under resource-constrained environment, model compression becomes a necessity, yet existing methods typically apply uniform compression ratios, overlooking the heterogeneity in user intent complexity. This "one-size-fits-all" approach leads to a fundamental Capacity-Complexity Mismatch, where computational resources are either wasted on simple instances or insufficient for complex ones. To address this, we propose RACE (Ranking-uncertainty Aware Capacity-Adaptive Customization for Efficient Sequential Recommendation), a framework that dynamically adapts both model structures and input sequences with uncertainty-aware compression ratios. To ensure capacity-adaptive inference, RACE captures the model's intrinsic uncertainty of its predictions and transforms it into a proactive resource allocator. Specifically, we derive a list-wise ranking-uncertainty metric from the distributional stability of top-$K$ predictions, which serves as a measurable proxy for instance complexity. A lightweight predictor is then trained via self-supervision to anticipate this uncertainty from early-layer features. 5. RecMem: Recurrent Memory Compression for Long-Sequence Generative Recommendation

Generative recommendation benefits from long interaction histories, but the cost of processing them directly grows with history length. We propose RecMem, a recurrent memory compression framework guided by three desiderata: space invariance, semantic fidelity, and lifelong evolvability. RecMem compresses historical segments into a fixed set of memory slots using a bifurcated encoder-decoder with shared lower layers and slot-wise forget-gated updates. Recommendation supervision trains the memory to preserve predictive information, while stream-consistency training aligns predictions from sequential and packed processing. By construction, the memory state and decoder context are independent of history length; under contraction and approximate semigroup consistency, we further derive a horizon-independent bound on the state discrepancy between these two processing orders. On MerRec, RecMem uses 16 memory slots and a 216-token decoder context, compared with 1,200 tokens for HSTU-1200, while retaining 87.9% of HSTU-1200's Recall@10 and 97.0% of its Recall@200. It outperforms PHSTU and the evaluated recent-only and retrieval baselines, and analyses of distant history show that archived interactions contribute predictive signal.
